Common Bookkeeping Mistakes (and how to fix them)

Healthy books make it easier to see cash flow, plan for taxes, and keep operations calm. Below are common pitfalls we see with small businesses, plus quick fixes you can apply today.

1. Mixing personal and business transactions

What it is: Using one bank account or credit card for both personal and business spending.

Why it matters: Blending transactions muddies records, increases audit risk, and makes cash flow unclear.

Quick fix: Open dedicated business accounts and update payment methods in apps and subscriptions.

2. Skipping regular reconciliations

What it is: Waiting months to match bank and credit card statements to the ledger.

Why it matters: Missing reconciliations hide duplicate charges, bank errors, or fraud until it is costly to unwind.

Quick fix: Reconcile weekly or monthly and set recurring reminders in your calendar or bookkeeping software.

3. Inconsistent categorization

What it is: Similar expenses are labeled differently from month to month.

Why it matters: Reports become unreliable, and tax deductions can be missed or questioned.

Quick fix: Create a short chart of accounts and stick to it; document rules for common vendors.

4. Poor documentation of receipts

What it is: Receipts, invoices, and mileage logs are missing or scattered.

Why it matters: Without support, expenses may be disallowed and it is harder to answer vendor or tax questions.

Quick fix: Snap photos and attach them to transactions in your app; store digital copies in a shared folder.

5. Overlooking sales tax and payroll liabilities

What it is: Sales tax, payroll tax, or franchise fees are not tracked as separate liabilities.

Why it matters: Funds can be spent accidentally, leading to penalties or cash crunches when payments are due.

Quick fix: Use liability accounts, run aging reports, and move tax funds to a separate savings account.

6. Misclassifying owner draws and contributions

What it is: Owner withdrawals or personal payments into the business are treated like income or expenses.

Why it matters: Financial statements become misleading, affecting profit decisions and lender conversations.

Quick fix: Post owner-related transactions to equity accounts labeled Draws and Contributions.

7. Ignoring accounts receivable follow-up

What it is: Invoices are sent but not monitored for timely payment.

Why it matters: Cash flow stalls and collections take longer the more time passes.

Quick fix: Send friendly reminders before due dates, automate late notices, and review open invoices weekly.

8. Not backing up or restricting access

What it is: Accounting files are stored on one device or shared broadly without roles or permissions.

Why it matters: Data loss or accidental edits can disrupt operations and delay reporting.

Quick fix: Enable cloud backups, use user roles in your software, and keep an admin log of changes.

Pro tip: A brief quarterly review with a bookkeeper can reveal small issues before they create tax-time stress.

Fast checklist

  • Separate business bank and credit accounts are in use.
  • Bank and card reconciliations are completed monthly.
  • Chart of accounts and vendor rules are documented.
  • Receipts and invoices are stored with transactions.
  • Tax liabilities are tracked and reserved in a dedicated fund.
  • Owner draws and contributions hit equity, not income/expense.
  • Open invoices are reviewed weekly and reminders are scheduled.
  • Bookkeeping software access is role-based and backed up.

This is general information and not tax advice.
